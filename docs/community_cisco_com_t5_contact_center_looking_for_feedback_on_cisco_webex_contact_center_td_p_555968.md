We have evaluated Webex Contact Center together with Webex Calling and Dedicated Instance as part of our cloud contact-center and enterprise voice testing. Our experience is mainly from POC, integration, and operational testing rather than a complete large-scale production migration, but several things stood out.
**Implementation**
The basic platform configuration in Control Hub is straightforward. The real effort is documenting and rebuilding the existing operational logic:
Entry points, queues, skills, and routing flows  
Business hours, holidays, callbacks, and announcements  
PSTN connectivity and number normalization  
Call recording, CRM, workforce management, and reporting integrations  
Emergency calling, failover, and support ownership
A phased pilot is much safer than moving everything at once. We started with controlled users and call flows so we could compare voice quality, routing behavior, reporting, and agent experience before expanding.
**Stability and reliability**
Our testing was stable, and voice quality was good. In our environment, Webex Calling Dedicated Instance also provided better and more consistent audio than some of our Microsoft Teams Direct Routing tests.
The cloud model removes much of the server maintenance associated with UCCE, such as operating-system upgrades, database maintenance, certificates, and application patching. However, you still need strong monitoring around PSTN carriers, CUBE connectivity, integrations, recordings, and agent network quality.
**Agent and supervisor experience**
The browser-based Agent Desktop is cleaner and easier for new users than a heavily customized legacy desktop. Agents can manage voice and digital interactions from one workspace, and supervisors have access to team and performance information (Cisco, “Understand Your Agent Desktop”).
The main challenge is change management. Supervisors and agents should be involved early because even small differences in transfer behavior, wrap-up codes, call controls, and reporting can generate resistance during migration.
**Reporting and analytics**
Analyzer provides useful real-time and historical reporting, and supervisors can access agent statistics directly from the desktop (Cisco, “View and Manage Agent Performance Reports”).
That said, organizations coming from UCCE and CUIC should not assume that every custom report will have a direct replacement. We currently rely heavily on SQL and custom CUIC reports for operational questions such as RONA analysis, agent timelines, queue reconciliation, and failed-call evidence.
For Webex Contact Center, evaluate early whether Analyzer, APIs, scheduled exports, or an external reporting platform can reproduce your critical reports. Reporting should be part of the POC, not something addressed after migration.
**Workforce management**
Webex WFO includes workforce management, quality management, and analytics capabilities (Cisco, “Webex Workforce Optimization”).
Organizations already using Calabrio, Alvaria, or another WFM platform should validate forecasting data, schedules, adherence, recording imports, user synchronization, and historical retention. Existing processes may not transfer one-for-one, and licensing should be reviewed carefully.
**AI capabilities**
The AI roadmap is one of the platform’s strongest areas. Cisco offers capabilities such as interaction summaries, real-time assistance, automated guidance, call-drop summaries, and supervisor insights (Cisco, “Cisco AI Assistant for Webex Contact Center”).
I would still treat AI as a later phase. First make sure routing, recording, reporting, integrations, and data quality are stable. You also need clear decisions around customer consent, transcription, retention, supported languages, and AI governance.
**Support and lessons learned**
The most important lesson is to establish ownership before deployment. When an issue involves Webex Contact Center, CUBE, a PSTN carrier, CRM, recording, or WFM, it can take time to determine which vendor owns the failure.
Have a strong Cisco partner, documented escalation paths, and repeatable test cases with timestamps, calling numbers, queue names, and interaction IDs.
Overall, I see Webex Contact Center as a strong option for organizations that want to reduce on-premises infrastructure and adopt cloud analytics, digital channels, and AI. However, the decision should be based on a real POC using your most complicated call flows and reports—not only a vendor demonstration.
