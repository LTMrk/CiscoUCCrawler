Hi UM,
A 403 here means the request was authenticated but not authorized. That can be a missing scope on the token, insufficient admin privileges, or another authorization mismatch. Since your personal token works and the xAPI call succeeds, a missing scope in the OAuth grant is the most likely cause, but treat it as something to verify. Org wide device listing with GET /v1/devices requires spark-admin:devices_read.
Your personal token works because it operates on your behalf as a testing token. A production OAuth token only gets the scopes included and approved in its grant, so the issue is what the integration token actually carries, not your admin rights.
The most likely reason it does not carry the scope: when you add a scope to an integration, existing tokens do not gain it. You have to run the OAuth flow again so the admin consents to the new scope, then get fresh tokens. The best place to confirm is the scope field in the token response, which shows exactly what was issued. That also explains why xAPI works (spark:xapi_statuses) while /v1/devices does not.
So confirm spark-admin:devices_read is on the app and in the authorization request, authorize again as your admin user, check the returned scope field, and use the new token.
For long lived, org wide automation that should not depend on one user's account lifecycle, a Service App is a better fit than an Integration, worth considering as this grows.
Refs: Webex Devices API, <https://developer.webex.com/devices/docs/devices> and Understanding the OAuth flow of a Webex Integration, <https://developer.webex.com/docs/understanding-oauth-flow-of-webex-integration>
I am not a Cisco employee. If it helped, please mark it as the solution or leave a thumbs up.
